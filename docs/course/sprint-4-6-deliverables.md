# Sprint 4–6 deliverable briefs

*Transcribed from the Sprint 4, 5 and 6 assignments on Canvas on 2026-10-22; Canvas and its rubric are authoritative. Dates are from the [README](../../README.md) calendar except where Canvas gives one.*

All three sprints point back to [project-spec.md](project-spec.md) and [ai-guidelines.md](ai-guidelines.md). The team uses Dennis, Wixom, Roth & Haley, *Systems Analysis and Design*, 8th ed. (Wiley, 2021) as the method reference for every remaining deliverable (team decision, 2026-10-22).

## Sprint 4 (Nov 2–8): usable, testable, explainable

**Purpose.** Demonstrate that the system can be used, tested and understood by its intended audience.

By the end of the sprint the team should have:

- A working system with enough functionality to conduct a training session.
- A Specification document with documented test cases and results.
- AI Decision Log.
- Change Log.

**Quality expectations.** Artifacts should reflect a system that is usable, testable and explainable, not just technically functional.

## Sprint 5 (Nov 16–22; due Nov 22, 11:59 pm; 130 points on Canvas): ready for real use

**Purpose.** Prepare the system for real-world use by addressing how it will be deployed, secured and operated beyond the classroom.

By the end of the sprint the team should have:

- Training and user manual.
- AI Decision Log.
- Change Log.

**Quality expectations.** Plans should be realistic, internally consistent and appropriate for the scale and sensitivity of the system. Focus on clarity of decisions and justification of tradeoffs rather than exhaustive technical detail.

## Sprint 6 (Nov 30–Dec 9): complete and coherent

**Purpose.** Conclude the project by delivering a complete, coherent and defensible system and documentation set that reflects learning, iteration and feedback across all prior sprints.

By the end of the sprint the team should have:

- The final working system, incorporating feedback and refinements from all previous sprints.
- The final version of every project deliverable, revised for accuracy, consistency and completeness.
- A post-project review, reflecting on outcomes, decisions and lessons learned.

**Quality expectations.** All deliverables should be internally consistent, professionally presented and represent the team's best work. Earlier artifacts should not appear "frozen in time"; they should reflect how the system actually evolved and was implemented.

## Sprint deliverable rubric (same for Sprints 4, 5 and 6)

| Criterion | Points | What it evaluates | Exemplary looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alignment | 20 | Whether the work addresses the client's business problem, stakeholder needs, constraints and the sprint's purpose: working on the right problem, not technical perfection. | Consistently focused on the client's actual problem; priorities, scope and constraints visible in what was chosen; competing needs and scope questions handled thoughtfully. "Developing" is work driven by the checklist or the available technology rather than client needs. |
| Judgment | 40 | Why the team made its choices about requirements, priorities, models, designs, technology and AI content. | Decisions explicit and supported by client, stakeholder, project, testing or other credible evidence; evidence distinguished from assumptions; alternatives and tradeoffs considered; AI output evaluated with independent accept/modify/reject decisions. "Developing" rationales restate the decision, lean on generic best practice, or treat client statements, assumptions or AI output as sufficient. |
| Execution | 30 | How competently the chosen approach was carried out (models, prototypes, specifications, functionality, testing, deployment plans, training materials). | Complete, accurate, usable; artifacts technically credible and mutually consistent; works as intended with no material errors, gaps or contradictions. |
| Revision | 10 | Response to feedback, new evidence, testing and downstream changes. | Feedback evaluated systematically; targeted improvements; Change Log says what changed, why and what prompted it; rejected feedback or "no change needed" has a convincing rationale; revisions carried through every affected deliverable. "Incomplete" includes resubmitting prior work unchanged. |

Ratings run Exemplary, Proficient, Developing, Incomplete. Total 100 points per rubric.
