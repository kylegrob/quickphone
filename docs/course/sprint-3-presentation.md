# Sprint 3 presentation brief

*Transcribed from the Sprint 3 assignment on Canvas on 2026-10-22; the Canvas copy and its rubric are authoritative. Sprint 3 runs Oct 19–25, 2026.*

## What to present

- **Show what changed.** Briefly restate the problem and explain the most important revisions since Sprint 2 and what prompted them.
- **Defend the acquisition and technical approach.** Use the alternative matrix and hardware/software specifications to explain why the selected approach fits the project's requirements and constraints.
- **Curate the interface.** Compare at least three interface alternatives, summarize sponsor feedback, defend the selected design, and explain what you retained or rejected from the other options. Address user fit and accessibility.
- **Critique the physical models.** Use the physical DFDs and physical ERD to identify what AI produced, what your team corrected, and why the models now support implementation.
- **Demonstrate value and reflect.** Show the prototype or Sprint 3 PSPI, connect it to stakeholder outcomes, identify unresolved design risks, and request feedback for Sprint 4.

## Use these as evidence

- Acquisition alternative matrix and hardware/software specifications
- Three or more interface alternatives, sponsor feedback, and selected prototype
- Physical DFDs and physical ERD
- Current product backlog, Sprint 3 backlog, PSPI, Change Log, and AI Decision Log

Select only the evidence that advances your argument. You do not need to show every artifact in full.

## Assessment criteria

The same five criteria apply in every sprint:

| Criterion | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Whether the presentation addresses the client problem, sprint purpose, and audience needs. |
| Explanation | Whether major decisions are clearly explained and defended, including decisions about AI contributions. |
| Value Proposition | Whether evidence shows who benefits and how the team's decisions create meaningful outcomes. |
| Reflection | Whether the team honestly addresses limitations, risks, uncertainty, feedback, and next steps. |
| Delivery | Whether the presentation is clear, concise, professional, visually supported, and appropriately timed. |

Ratings: Exemplary, Proficient, Developing, and Incomplete. See the Canvas rubric for the full performance-level descriptions.

## Essential requirements

- All team members must participate and be prepared to answer questions.
- Show comparison and critique, not only the final design.
- Use the prototype and models as evidence for your claims.
- Submit slides and supporting materials to Canvas by the deadline.
- Make all prototypes, models, and visuals legible for the audience.
