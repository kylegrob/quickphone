# Sprint 1–3 review against the textbook and feedback

Reviewed 2026-10-22 (Claude Code, Claude Opus 5.5) against Dennis, Wixom, Roth & Haley, *Systems Analysis and Design* 8e (cited as SAD 8e with chapter and figure), the Sprint 2 professor feedback, [project-spec.md](course/project-spec.md) and the [Sprint 3 brief](course/sprint-3-presentation.md). It covers the current version of each deliverable listed in [README.md](README.md), mostly the Sprint 3 drafts. These are findings for the team to accept or reject. **Status (Oct 22):** A6, B1, B5, B6, B8 and C2–C6 were applied at the user's request (logged in both logs, decisions pending); the rest are still open.

**Overall.** The Sprint 2 feedback was answered carefully: every item in `feedback/sprint-2.md` has a visible fix, the requirement → use case → DFD → entity trace (Models Table 7.2) is strong, and the feasibility arithmetic reconciles (NPV $17,082, ROI 353%, payback 2.5 months, every sensitivity row). The risk for the Oct 25 submission is not quality but **missing Sprint 3 evidence**: no acquisition alternative matrix, no hardware/software specification, and only one interface design rather than three alternatives with sponsor feedback. Second, a handful of textbook rules are still broken (nine Level 0 processes, use cases without pre/postconditions, derived attributes in a "3NF" ERD). Third, there is stale text in the revised Sprint 1 documents that a grader reading "not frozen in time" will notice.

| B1 | **Level 0 has nine processes.** Showing it in three parts helps readability but does not change the count. | SAD 8e Fig. 4-24: "between three and seven processes per DFD"; AGENTS.md | Team decision: merge to seven (candidates: 5.0 + 6.0 as one completion-and-release process; 3.0 + 4.0 as one bench process), or keep nine and defend it explicitly in the models document and presentation. Either way, say why. |
| B2 | **Use cases have no preconditions or postconditions.** | project-spec Use cases ("must include… preconditions… postconditions"); SAD 8e Ch. 4 use case form (postconditions of one use case are the preconditions of the next) | Add both to all twelve. | B3 | Triggers labelled **temporal** are status-change events (UC-03, 04, 05, 08, 09). | SAD 8e Ch. 4: temporal triggers are time-based (payroll is due); "inventory levels reaching the reorder point" is its example of an *external* trigger | Relabel as external; no use case here is truly temporal. |
| B4 | UC-12 → UC-01 is described as "uses". | SAD 8e Ch. 4 relationships: association, include, extend, generalization | Model it as an extend (the appointment path extends check-in) or drop the relationship and keep the 1a alternate flow. |
| B5 | **Logical ERD is not fully 3NF**: QUOTE.TotalAmount is LaborAmount + PartsAmount; REPAIR_JOB.Status repeats the latest JOB_STATUS_HISTORY row; STORE_PART_STOCK.QuantityOnHand is the running sum of INVENTORY_TXN. | project-spec Data models ("3rd normal form"); SAD 8e Appendix 5A Fig. 5A-1: 3NF removes "the transitive dependency or derived attribute" | Remove them from the logical ERD; keep them in the physical ERD as documented denormalizations for speed (SAD 8e Ch. 10, Optimizing Access Speed). | B6 | The **logical ERD already carries physical detail** (int/string types, surrogate integer keys, CK markers), so logical and physical differ only in naming. | SAD 8e Ch. 10, Moving from Logical to Physical Data Models | Strip types and checked-value markers from the logical diagrams. |
| B7 | CRUD grid is store-level; no entity-level CRUD matrix. | SAD 8e Ch. 5 Fig. 5-16 and Ch. 10, Revisiting the CRUD Matrix | Entity × process CRUD matrix. It will also show that nothing is ever deleted (accounts and parts are deactivated), which should be stated. |
| B8 | Level 0 part 1 has many crossing flows, and several labels are struck through by lines ("Matching customer records", "Booked appointments"). | SAD 8e Fig. 4-24: "a minimum number of data flow lines cross"; feedback item 6 on readability | Re-route part 1 (stores to the right of the processes they serve, D1 next to 2.0). |
| B9 | No nonfunctional requirement covers **performance** or **cultural/political** needs. Rejecting the AI's invented targets was right; leaving the category empty is not. | SAD 8e Ch. 3 nonfunctional types (operational, performance, security, cultural and political) | Ask the owner for one counter-speed expectation (e.g. find a job while the customer waits) and record either a target or "none required, owner confirmed". |
